Build Version 2.3 — Analyst Reasoning and Decision Invalidation Layer.

Goal:
Make every trade recommendation read like a disciplined analyst assessment rather than a list of calculations.

Important:
- Paper trading and research only.
- No real order execution.
- Do not change existing strategy rules, similarity logic, portfolio logic, or learning safeguards.
- This layer should improve reasoning, explanation, and decision monitoring.
- Keep every conclusion deterministic, auditable, and based on available data.
- Do not invent facts or explanations that are not supported by the underlying metrics.

1. Create backend module

Create:

backend/analyst_reasoning.py

Responsibilities:

- Read the complete decision output.
- Summarize current technical conditions.
- Summarize historical similarity evidence.
- Explain confidence increases and reductions.
- Identify conflicting evidence.
- Identify what would invalidate the current decision.
- Identify what would strengthen the current decision.
- Produce a concise analyst summary.

2. Four-part reasoning structure

For every stock, generate these sections:

A. What the system sees now

Include:

- Current trend
- Momentum
- Volume
- Volatility
- Market regime
- Sector strength
- Risk/reward
- Data quality
- Open-position status

Example:

“WIPRO is trading below EMA20 and EMA50, momentum is weakening, volume is below confirmation level, and the broader market is Neutral.”

B. What happened in similar historical setups

Include only Historical Similarity Evidence:

- Match count
- Average similarity
- Win rate
- Expectancy
- Profit factor
- Average return
- Expected drawdown
- Average holding period
- Evidence reliability

Example:

“Among 50 similar historical setups with 82% average similarity, only 35% were profitable. Expectancy was -0.50% and profit factor was 0.75.”

C. Why this recommendation was made

Explain:

- Which factor had the greatest impact
- Which evidence supported the recommendation
- Which evidence contradicted it
- Why the final recommendation remained BUY, WATCH, EXIT, or AVOID
- The exact source of every adjustment

Example:

“The technical exit signal was already weak, and negative similarity evidence reduced confidence by another 7 points. Although the descriptive Pattern Knowledge result was mildly positive, it did not affect confidence.”

D. What would invalidate or change the decision

Generate specific measurable conditions.

For BUY or STRONG BUY, show downside invalidation conditions such as:

- Price closes below stop-loss
- EMA trend turns bearish
- RSI falls below a threshold
- MACD turns bearish
- Volume confirmation disappears
- Sector ranking weakens
- Market regime changes
- Expected risk/reward falls below minimum

For WATCH or AVOID, show upgrade conditions such as:

- Price closes above EMA20 and EMA50
- RSI improves into target range
- MACD histogram turns positive
- Volume ratio exceeds threshold
- Opportunity score crosses minimum
- Similarity expectancy turns positive
- Market or sector strength improves

For EXIT, show reversal conditions such as:

- Bearish exit signal is cancelled
- Price reclaims trend level
- Momentum recovers
- Stop or target logic changes

Each condition must include:

- Metric
- Current value
- Trigger value
- Direction
- Why it matters

3. Decision monitoring state

For every stock return:

- Current recommendation
- Confidence
- Decision timestamp
- Decision expiry
- Current invalidation status
- Number of invalidation conditions met
- Number of upgrade conditions met
- Decision state:
  - VALID
  - WEAKENING
  - INVALIDATED
  - IMPROVING
  - EXPIRED

4. Decision expiry

Add a validity window.

For swing trades:

- Default decision validity: until the next completed daily candle
- Open-position exit decisions remain valid until resolved or superseded
- Recalculate after meaningful data changes

Display:

“Valid until next daily close”

or

“Re-evaluation required”

5. Conflict detection

Detect and show conflicts such as:

- Technical evidence positive but similarity evidence negative
- Pattern Knowledge positive but live technical data weak
- Strong confidence but low data quality
- High expectancy but poor risk/reward
- Good individual setup but weak sector or market

Classify conflict severity:

- LOW
- MEDIUM
- HIGH

Example:

“High conflict: technical conditions are improving, but 50 similar historical setups had negative expectancy.”

6. Analyst summary

Generate a concise summary of no more than 120 words.

Format:

Recommendation:
Confidence:
Primary reason:
Historical evidence:
Main risk:
What would change the decision:

Example:

“WATCH, confidence 61%. Technical momentum is improving, but current volume remains weak. Forty-two similar historical setups had slightly positive expectancy but only medium reliability. The main risk is a bearish market-regime shift. Upgrade to BUY only if price closes above EMA50 with volume above 1.5× average and risk/reward remains above 2:1.”

10. Do not confuse correlation with causation

Root Cause Analysis must be labelled as:

- Associated factor
- Probable contributor
- Not proven causal

Do not state that a factor caused a loss unless directly supported by deterministic trade logic, such as stop-loss hit.

11. Data quality safety

When live data is unavailable:

- State clearly that the decision is provisional.
- Do not generate BUY or STRONG BUY.
- Do not present fallback data as current market evidence.
- Set decision state to DATA_LIMITED.
- Show the missing-data fields.

12. Automated tests

Test:

- Correct reasoning source attribution
- No contradiction between sections
- Invalidation threshold generation
- Upgrade threshold generation
- Conflict detection
- Decision expiry
- Data-limited behavior
- EXIT reasoning for open positions
- No unsupported causal claims
- Deterministic summaries

Do not add a separate navigation page.
Enhance Trade Decisions and Portfolio Manager only.
