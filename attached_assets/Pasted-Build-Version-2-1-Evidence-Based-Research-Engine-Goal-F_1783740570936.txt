Build Version 2.1 — Evidence-Based Research Engine.

Goal:
For every stock, compare the current market setup with historically similar setups from the Historical Knowledge Base and use that evidence to improve the final recommendation and confidence.

Important:
- Paper trading and research only.
- No real orders.
- Do not replace the existing strategy engine.
- Similarity evidence is an additional decision layer.
- Keep all calculations deterministic, explainable, auditable, and reproducible.
- Never use synthetic/mock records as historical evidence.
- Do not issue BUY or STRONG BUY when current market data is fallback/mock/unavailable.

1. Create backend module

Create:

backend/similarity_engine.py

Responsibilities:

- Read the current stock setup.
- Search historical_knowledge_trades.
- Calculate similarity between the current setup and every eligible historical trade.
- Return the best matching historical examples.
- Calculate evidence statistics from the matches.

3. Similarity score

Calculate a weighted similarity score from 0 to 100.

Default weights:

- Strategy match: 15
- Sector match: 10
- Market regime match: 12
- Volatility regime match: 8
- RSI similarity: 8
- ADX similarity: 8
- MACD state match: 8
- EMA alignment match: 10
- VWAP state match: 5
- Supertrend state match: 5
- ATR similarity: 4
- Volume-ratio similarity: 4
- Momentum direction match: 3

Total: 100

Rules:

- Exact categorical match receives full points.
- Related regime match may receive partial points.
- Numerical features use normalized distance.
- Missing feature values receive zero contribution and reduce evidence quality.
- The score calculation must be documented and testable.

4. Historical match retrieval

For each current stock:

- Search only real Yahoo Finance historical simulations.
- Exclude the current/future period.
- Prevent lookahead bias.
- Return the top 50 matches with similarity >= 65%.
- Use the top 20 most similar matches for the primary evidence calculation.
- Also return the top 5 examples for display.

If fewer than 10 valid matches exist:
- Mark evidence as LOW.
- Do not increase confidence.
- Allow only a small negative adjustment when the evidence is consistently poor.

6. Evidence reliability

Classify evidence:

- Fewer than 10 matches = VERY LOW
- 10–19 matches = LOW
- 20–49 matches = MEDIUM
- 50+ matches = HIGH

Also reduce reliability when:

- Average similarity is below 70%.
- Important current features are missing.
- The matches are concentrated in only one stock.
- The evidence comes from a very narrow time period.

Display the reason for the reliability classification.

7. Evidence-based confidence adjustment

Start from the existing final confidence before similarity evidence.

Positive adjustment:

If:
- At least 20 matches
- Average similarity >= 75%
- Expectancy > +0.75%
- Profit factor >= 1.5

Then increase confidence gradually by +2 to +10.

Negative adjustment:

If:
- At least 20 matches
- Average similarity >= 75%
- Expectancy < 0
- Profit factor < 1.0

Then reduce confidence gradually by -3 to -15.

Rules:

- Maximum similarity adjustment: +10 / -15.
- Final confidence remains between 5 and 95.
- Similarity evidence cannot override hard risk filters.
- Similarity evidence cannot create BUY solely by itself.
- Low or very-low reliability evidence cannot increase confidence.
- Every adjustment must include a plain-English explanation.

12. Prevent common data errors

- Do not compare current records with future trades.
- Do not include duplicate historical trades.
- Do not use mock or fallback records.
- Do not mix long and short trades.
- Do not compare intraday trades with swing trades.
- Do not compare records with incompatible holding periods unless clearly labelled as partial matches.
- Do not treat missing data as a positive match.
