## 1. Stock Selection: The Foundation Before Any Trade

Before any entry logic applies, experienced intraday traders filter their universe down to a small watchlist of high-probability stocks. The wrong stock makes even the best strategy fail.

### 1.1 The Three Pillars of Selection

| Pillar | What It Means | How Traders Assess It |
|--------|--------------|----------------------|
| **Liquidity** | Ability to enter/exit quickly without moving the price | Daily volume > 5 lakh shares, tight bid-ask spread, deep order book |
| **Volatility** | Enough price movement to capture profit, but not so erratic that risk becomes unmanageable | Stocks that move 2–5% daily; ATR (Average True Range) analysis |
| **Correlation** | How the stock moves relative to its sector and the broader market | Sector ETF trend alignment; Nifty/Bank Nifty direction |

### 1.2 Practical Stock Selection Rules

- **Choose liquid stocks:** High-volume stocks allow quick entry and exit without significant price impact. A tighter bid-ask spread signifies better liquidity.
- **Avoid extreme volatility:** Stocks with violent, unpredictable swings make timing nearly impossible. Moderate volatility is the sweet spot.
- **Follow sector trends:** If banking stocks are seeing strong buying, individual banking counters often continue attracting attention. Trade in the direction of the strongest sector.
- **Prefer F&O-listed stocks:** Stocks traded in the derivatives segment typically have better liquidity and higher volumes.
- **News-sensitive stocks:** Earnings reports, regulatory developments, and macroeconomic news create intraday catalysts—but require tighter risk control.
- **High daily turnover:** Reflects strong market activity and ensures reliable execution.

> **Key Insight:** A SEBI study found that 7 out of 10 individual intraday traders in the equity cash segment incurred losses in FY23—making disciplined stock selection not just a strategy, but a necessity for survival.

## 2. Pre-Market Routine & Daily Timeline

Experienced traders do not "wing it." They follow a structured daily routine.

### 2.1 Pre-Market (Before 9:15 AM IST / 9:30 AM ET)

1. **Review overnight global markets** — US futures, Asian markets, commodity prices.
2. **Scan for news catalysts** — Earnings announcements, FDA approvals, M&A news, economic data releases.
3. **Build a watchlist** — 5–10 stocks that meet liquidity + volatility + news criteria.
4. **Mark key levels** — Previous day high/low, pre-market high/low, support/resistance zones, VWAP anchor points.
5. **Check sector strength** — Which sectors are leading/lagging? Trade with sector momentum, not against it.

### 2.2 Market Open (First 15–30 Minutes)

- Many experienced traders **wait 15–25 minutes after the open** before taking any trade. The first minutes are often chaotic, with false breakouts and whipsaws.
- The opening range (first 15–30 minutes) establishes the day's initial balance. Breaking above/below this range often sets the directional bias.
- Volume is highest near the open; breakouts here carry more weight than breakouts later in the session.

### 2.3 Mid-Session (10:30 AM – 2:00 PM)

- Lower volatility period for many stocks. Momentum strategies may see fewer setups.
- Mean-reversion strategies (buying dips to VWAP, selling rallies) often work better here.
- Many traders reduce position sizes or step away during this "dead zone."

### 2.4 Power Hour (Last 60–90 Minutes)

- Volume and volatility often pick up again.
- Institutional rebalancing creates directional moves.
- Traders look for late-session breakouts or reversals.
- **All intraday positions must be closed before market close** to avoid overnight gap risk.

## 3. Charts, Timeframes & Multi-Timeframe Analysis

Professional intraday traders use a **multi-timeframe approach** to confirm setups and avoid noise.

### 3.1 The Standard Timeframe Stack

| Timeframe | Purpose | What Traders Look For |
|-----------|---------|----------------------|
| **Daily** | Trend direction & key levels | 20 EMA, 50 EMA, support/resistance, overall trend bias |
| **15-Minute** | Session structure & swing points | Opening range, higher highs/lows, momentum shifts |
| **5-Minute** | Entry/exit precision & pattern confirmation | Candlestick patterns, VWAP tests, breakout confirmations |
| **1-Minute** | Exact timing for entry/exit | Micro-structure, order flow, precise stop placement |

### 3.2 How Professionals Use Multiple Timeframes

- **Top-down analysis:** Start with the daily chart to determine if the stock is in an uptrend, downtrend, or range. Only take longs in uptrends, shorts in downtrends (with rare exceptions for counter-trend scalps).
- **15-minute for context:** Identify the session's structure—are we making higher highs and higher lows (bullish) or lower highs and lower lows (bearish)?
- **5-minute for setups:** Most intraday patterns (flags, triangles, VWAP bounces) are traded on the 5-minute chart for reliability.
- **1-minute for execution:** Use the 1-minute chart only for fine-tuning entries and placing precise stop-losses. Trading solely on 1-minute charts produces excessive noise.

> **Pro Tip:** Candlestick patterns tend to work better on 5-minute and 15-minute charts than on 1-minute charts, where noise dominates.

## 4. Entry Logic: What Experienced Traders Look For Before Buying

Before pulling the trigger, experienced traders run through a systematic checklist. No checklist = no trade.

### 4.1 The 5-Step Entry Test

1. **Trend Alignment** — Is the stock trending in the direction of the higher timeframe? (Daily trend = filter)
2. **Key Level Interaction** — Is price at a meaningful support/resistance level, VWAP, or moving average?
3. **Setup Confirmation** — Has a valid pattern formed? (Breakout, pullback, reversal candlestick, etc.)
4. **Volume Confirmation** — Is volume increasing on the move? Low-volume breakouts often fail.
5. **Risk/Reward Validation** — Does the setup offer at least 1.5:1 to 2:1 reward-to-risk? If not, skip it.

### 4.2 What They Look For Before Buying (Long Entry)

| Factor | Bullish Condition |
|--------|-------------------|
| **Price vs. VWAP** | Price above VWAP with VWAP sloping upward |
| **Moving Averages** | Price above 9 EMA and 20 EMA; 9 EMA > 20 EMA |
| **Trend Structure** | Higher highs and higher lows on 5-min/15-min chart |
| **Support Level** | Bounce off prior resistance-turned-support, daily support, or rising trendline |
| **Volume** | Above-average volume on the entry candle |
| **Candlestick** | Bullish engulfing, hammer at support, morning star, or marubozu |
| **RSI** | Between 40–60 (not overbought), or bouncing from oversold (<30) |
| **MACD** | Bullish crossover or histogram expanding above zero line |
| **Sector** | Sector ETF is green and leading the market |
| **Market** | Nifty/S&P 500 is trending up or at least not selling off hard |

### 4.3 What They Look For Before Shorting (Short Entry)

| Factor | Bearish Condition |
|--------|-------------------|
| **Price vs. VWAP** | Price below VWAP with VWAP sloping downward |
| **Moving Averages** | Price below 9 EMA and 20 EMA; 9 EMA < 20 EMA |
| **Trend Structure** | Lower highs and lower lows on 5-min/15-min chart |
| **Resistance Level** | Rejection at prior support-turned-resistance, daily resistance, or falling trendline |
| **Volume** | Above-average volume on the breakdown candle |
| **Candlestick** | Bearish engulfing, shooting star at resistance, evening star, or bearish marubozu |
| **RSI** | Between 60–80 (not oversold), or rolling over from overbought (>70) |
| **MACD** | Bearish crossover or histogram expanding below zero line |
| **Sector** | Sector ETF is red and lagging the market |
| **Market** | Nifty/S&P 500 is trending down or weak |

## 5. Core Intraday Strategies & Their Entry Rules

### 5.1 Opening Range Breakout (ORB)

One of the most widely used intraday strategies.

**Setup:**
- Define the opening range as the first 15 or 30 minutes of the regular session.
- Mark the Opening Range High (ORH) and Opening Range Low (ORL).

**Entry Rules:**
- **Long:** Price breaks above ORH with volume. Enter on the break or on a pullback retest of ORH as support.
- **Short:** Price breaks below ORL with volume. Enter on the break or on a pullback retest of ORL as resistance.

**Filters to Improve Win Rate:**
- Only take ORB if the overnight gap is reasonable (not too large—move may already be done).
- Only take longs if daily close > 20-day SMA; only shorts if daily close < 20-day SMA.
- After the break, price should stay on the breakout side of VWAP.
- Do not take breakouts after 11:30 AM ET—late breakouts are often head-fakes.

**Win Rate:** Typically 40–55%, depending on filters. Without a trend filter, closer to 40%. With filters, closer to 55%.

### 5.2 VWAP Bounce / Reversion Strategy

VWAP (Volume-Weighted Average Price) acts as an intraday fair-value benchmark.

**Setup:**
- In a trending day, price often pulls back to VWAP before continuing the trend.
- In a sideways day, price oscillates around VWAP.

**Entry Rules:**
- **Trend Continuation:** In an uptrend, wait for price to pull back to VWAP and form a bullish reversal candle (hammer, engulfing). Enter on confirmation.
- **Mean Reversion:** In a sideways market, buy when price closes in a statistically significant support zone below VWAP; target VWAP line.

**Exit Rules:**
- Stop-loss: Below the reversal candle low or outside the VWAP deviation band.
- Target: VWAP line for mean reversion; next resistance level for trend continuation.

**Pro Tip:** VWAP works best when combined with RSI (for overbought/oversold) and MACD (for momentum direction).

### 5.3 Breakout Strategy

**Setup:**
- Price clears a well-defined resistance level (or support for shorts) on increased volume.
- The more times price has tested the level, the more significant the breakout.

**Entry Rules:**
- Enter long after price closes above resistance on above-average volume.
- Enter short after price closes below support on above-average volume.
- Some traders wait for a retest of the broken level to enter with a tighter stop.

**Exit Rules:**
- Stop: Below the breakout level (for longs) or above the breakdown level (for shorts).
- Target: Measured move = height of the consolidation range projected from the breakout point.

### 5.4 Pullback to Moving Averages (The "Bone Zone")

Popular among trend-following day traders.

**Setup:**
- In a strong trend, price rarely stays far from its moving averages.
- The zone between the 9 EMA and 20 EMA acts as a dynamic support (in uptrends) or resistance (in downtrends).

**Entry Rules:**
- In an uptrend, wait for price to pull back into the 9–20 EMA zone.
- Look for a bullish reversal candle or consolidation within the zone.
- Enter when price breaks above the consolidation high or the entry candle's high.

**Exit Rules:**
- Stop: Below the 20 EMA or below the low of the pullback candle.
- Target: Next resistance level or use a trailing stop along the 9 EMA.

### 5.5 Scalping Strategy

**Profile:** 50–200 trades per day, each lasting seconds to a few minutes.

**Entry Rules:**
- Exploit tiny price discrepancies in highly liquid stocks.
- Enter on micro-breakouts, level II/order flow imbalances, or rapid momentum bursts.
- Requires lightning-fast execution and ultra-low commissions.

**Exit Rules:**
- Take profit immediately when the trade is profitable (often just a few cents per share).
- Cut losses instantly if the trade goes against you—no hesitation.

> **Warning:** Scalping is mentally exhausting. Most scalpers don't last more than a year before moving to longer timeframes or quitting.

## 6. Exit Logic: When and How to Sell

Exits are arguably more important than entries. A good exit system turns mediocre entries into profits; a bad exit system turns great entries into losses.

### 6.1 Exit Categories

| Exit Type | Purpose | When to Use |
|-----------|---------|-------------|
| **Stop-Loss Exit** | Cap the loss when the thesis is wrong | Every trade, placed before entry |
| **Profit Target Exit** | Lock in gains at a predetermined level | When R:R is achieved or at resistance |
| **Trailing Stop Exit** | Let winners run while protecting profits | In strong trending moves |
| **Time-Based Exit** | Close if the trade hasn't worked within X minutes | When momentum hasn't materialized |
| **End-of-Day Exit** | Close all positions before market close | Mandatory for all intraday trades |
| **Technical Exit** | Exit on reversal signal (candlestick, indicator) | When price action contradicts the thesis |

### 6.2 The "Free Trade" Exit System

A professional approach used by experienced traders:

1. **Sell half the position** when the trade hits the initial risk/reward target (e.g., 2:1 or 3:1). This covers the original risk and pays you.
2. **Trail the remaining half** using a moving average (e.g., 9 EMA or 20 EMA) or a trailing stop. Move the stop with the EMAs until the trend breaks.

> **Why it works:** Once the first half is sold, the trade becomes "free"—you cannot lose money on it. This removes emotional pressure and allows you to let the remaining half capture extended moves.

### 6.3 Specific Exit Rules by Scenario

**For Losing Trades:**
- **Hard stop-loss:** Placed before entry, based on technical structure (below support, below VWAP, or 1.5× ATR). Never move the stop further away.
- **Time stop:** If the trade hasn't shown momentum within 20–25 minutes, close it. The statistical window for the setup may have expired.
- **Three-strike rule:** After three consecutive losses in one day, stop trading entirely. Return the next session at reduced size (0.2% risk).

**For Winning Trades:**
- **Partial exits:** Take 50% off at the first target, trail the rest.
- **Break-even stop:** Once the trade is 1R (one risk unit) in profit, move the stop to break-even.
- **Trailing along EMAs:** In a strong trend, trail the stop just below the 9 EMA or 20 EMA.
- **Reversal candle exit:** If a shooting star, bearish engulfing, or doji forms at resistance, exit the full position.
- **RSI exit:** If RSI hits overbought (>70) on a long trade or oversold (<30) on a short trade, consider exiting.
- **Large daily move exit:** If the stock has moved beyond its typical daily range (measured by ATR), take profits—mean reversion becomes likely.

### 6.4 Time-Based Exit Guidelines

| Trade Duration | Exit Logic |
|---------------|------------|
| **Scalp (< 5 min)** | Quick profit target or immediate stop. No room for hope. |
| **Momentum (5–30 min)** | If it's not working in 5 minutes, close it. If it IS working, commit to holding 30+ minutes with a trailing stop. |
| **Swing within day (30 min–2 hrs)** | Time-stop at 25 minutes if no peak momentum. Trail stop once 1R profit is reached. |
| **EOD hold (2+ hrs)** | Hold 50–75% of morning entries until the last 15 minutes of the day if the trend is intact. |

> **Key Data Insight:** Research shows that for many traders, the best exit is consistently 30–60 minutes after their actual exit on winning trades. This suggests traders often exit too early and should consider time-based trailing rules.

## 7. Risk Management: The Non-Negotiable Framework

Risk management is what separates the 4% of traders who survive from the 96% who fail.

### 7.1 The Three Layers of Risk Control

| Layer | Rule | Purpose |
|-------|------|---------|
| **Trade-Level Risk** | Risk no more than 1% of account per trade | Prevents any single trade from causing significant damage |
| **Daily Risk** | Daily loss limit = 3× per-trade risk (3% max) | Stops the bleeding on bad days |
| **Drawdown Management** | Pause trading if drawdown reaches 8–10% of starting capital | Prevents emotional revenge trading |

### 7.2 The 1% Rule

- Risk no more than **1% of your total account value on any single trade**.
- On a $50,000 account, that's $500 maximum risk per trade.
- This does NOT mean buying $500 worth of stock—it means positioning your stop-loss so that if hit, you lose no more than $500.
- **Example:** Buy 200 shares at $50 with a stop at $47.50. Risk = 200 × $2.50 = $500 (1% of $50,000).

### 7.3 Risk/Reward Ratio

| R:R Ratio | Breakeven Win Rate | Professional Standard |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------------|
| 1:1 | 50%+ | Too tight; no edge |
| 1.5:1 | 40%+ | Minimum acceptable |
| 2:1 | 33%+ | Common target |
| 3:1 | 25%+ | Preferred by many pros |

- **Minimum:** 1.5:1 reward-to-risk. At this ratio, you can win 50% of trades and still be profitable.
- **Professional preference:** 2:1 to 3:1. At 3:1, you can lose 6 out of 10 trades and still grow the account.
- **Reality check:** Research from broker data suggests the average completed day trade achieves closer to 1.5:1. Targeting extremely high ratios (5:1+) often drops win rates too low to be sustainable.

### 7.4 Daily Loss Limits

- **3 consecutive losses:** Stop trading for the day.
- **Daily loss cap:** 3% of account maximum. When hit, shut down the platform.
- **After a large red day:** Take time to process and journal. Return the next session at reduced size (0.2% risk) until execution is clean again.

## 8. Position Sizing & The 1% Rule

Position sizing is the mathematical bridge between risk parameters and actual trades.

### 8.1 The Formula

```
Position Size = Dollar Risk per Trade / (Entry Price − Stop Loss Price)
```

**Example:**
- Account: $30,000
- Risk per trade (1%): $300
- Entry: $45.00
- Stop-loss: $44.25
- Risk per share: $0.75
- Position size: $300 / $0.75 = **400 shares**
- Total position value: $18,000 (60% of account, but only 1% risk)

### 8.2 Position Sizing Cheat Sheet

For a $10,000 account risking 1% ($100 per trade):

| Stop Distance | Position Size (Shares) |
|--------------|------------------------|
| $0.10 | 1,000 |
| $0.20 | 500 |
| $0.50 | 200 |
| $1.00 | 100 |

> **Pro Tip:** Build and memorize a cheat sheet for your account size. When the stock is moving and adrenaline is high, you don't want to be doing division.

### 8.3 Adjusting for Volatility

- Use ATR to set stop distances. Professional traders typically set stops at **1.5× to 2× the current 5-minute ATR**.
- On volatile stocks, this means smaller position sizes but more reliable stops.
- Never use a fixed percentage stop (e.g., "2% below entry")—stops should be based on technical structure.

## 9. Technical Indicators Used by Professionals

Successful traders typically combine **2–4 complementary indicators** rather than relying on a single signal.

### 9.1 The Core Indicator Toolkit

| Indicator | Type | Best Use for Intraday |
|-----------|------|----------------------|
| **VWAP** | Volume-based benchmark | Intraday fair value; dynamic S/R; trend filter |
| **9 EMA & 20 EMA** | Trend | Trend direction; pullback zones; trailing stops |
| **RSI (14)** | Momentum | Overbought (>70) / oversold (<30); divergence signals |
| **MACD** | Momentum | Trend reversal confirmation; histogram strength |
| **Bollinger Bands** | Volatility | Overbought/oversold extremes; squeeze = breakout warning |
| **Volume** | Confirmation | Validates price moves; low-volume breakouts often fail |
| **ATR (14)** | Volatility | Stop-loss placement; position sizing |

### 9.2 How Professionals Combine Indicators

- **VWAP + RSI:** Identify overbought/oversold conditions around VWAP.
- **VWAP + MACD:** Confirm momentum direction before entries.
- **Moving Averages + RSI:** Filter entries in the direction of the trend; avoid buying when RSI is overbought in a downtrend.
- **Candlestick Patterns + Volume:** A bullish engulfing on high volume is far more reliable than one on low volume.
- **Bollinger Bands + RSI:** Price touching the upper band with RSI > 70 = strong reversal signal.

> **Important:** Indicators should confirm each other, not contradict. If price is above VWAP but MACD is bearish and RSI is rolling over, wait for clarity.

## 10. Candlestick Patterns for Entry & Exit

Candlestick patterns reveal market psychology in real time. However, **context matters more than the pattern itself**.

### 10.1 Most Reliable Patterns for Intraday

| Pattern | Type | Signal | Best Location |
|---------|------|--------|---------------|
| **Hammer** | Reversal | Bullish | At support after a decline |
| **Shooting Star** | Reversal | Bearish | At resistance after a rally |
| **Bullish Engulfing** | Reversal | Bullish | At support; strong buying pressure |
| **Bearish Engulfing** | Reversal | Bearish | At resistance; strong selling pressure |
| **Doji** | Indecision | Neutral/Reversal | At key levels; signals exhaustion |
| **Marubozu** | Conviction | Strong directional | Confirms momentum in trend direction |
| **Inside Bar** | Continuation/Breakout | Pending | Consolidation before expansion |
| **Bull Flag / Bear Flag** | Continuation | Trend resumption | After strong impulse move |

### 10.2 Entry/Exit Rules Using Candlesticks

**Entry Example (Bullish Engulfing):**
1. Stock in uptrend on daily chart.
2. Price pulls back to 20 EMA or VWAP on 5-minute chart.
3. Bullish engulfing candle forms at support with above-average volume.
4. Enter on break above the engulfing candle's high.
5. Stop-loss: Below the engulfing candle's low.
6. Target: Next resistance level or 2:1 R:R.

**Exit Example (Shooting Star at Resistance):**
1. Long position in profit, approaching a known resistance level.
2. Shooting star candle forms at resistance with wick rejection.
3. Volume drops or RSI > 70.
4. Exit full position or sell half and trail the rest.

> **Key Rule:** A hammer in the middle of nowhere is meaningless. A hammer at the previous day's low after a morning selloff—that's tradeable.

## 11. Trading Psychology & Discipline Rules

### 11.1 The Mental Framework

- **Trade like a business, not a casino.** Every trade is a probabilistic bet with an edge, not a gamble.
- **Process over outcome.** A well-executed losing trade is better than a poorly executed winning trade.
- **You cannot control the market; you can only control your risk.**

### 11.2 Hard Discipline Rules

| Rule | Rationale |
|------|-----------|
| **No trade without a stop-loss** | Placed before entry, not after. Mental stops don't count. |
| **Never move a stop-loss further away** | This is not risk management; it's avoiding reality. Studies show traders who move stops lose 2.7× more on average. |
| **No FOMO trades** | If you missed the entry, you missed it. Chasing leads to bad prices and poor R:R. |
| **No revenge trading** | After a loss, stick to the plan. Emotional trading destroys accounts. |
| **Size down after losses** | After a red day or three consecutive losses, reduce risk to 0.2–0.5%. |
| **If one trade can ruin your day emotionally or financially, you're trading too big.** | Position sizing is the thermostat of emotional control. |

### 11.3 The "One-and-Done" Rule

Many professional intraday traders cap themselves at:
- **One trade per side per session** (one long, one short).
- Or even **one trade total per day**.

This prevents overtrading on choppy days where multiple false breakouts can trigger repeated losses.

## 12. Key Takeaways & Summary Checklist

### The Intraday Trader's Pre-Trade Checklist

```
☐ Stock is highly liquid (tight spread, high volume)
☐ Volatility is moderate (2–5% daily range, not erratic)
☐ Sector and market trend align with the trade direction
☐ Daily chart confirms the broader trend
☐ Price is at a meaningful level (VWAP, EMA, S/R)
☐ A valid setup pattern is present (breakout, pullback, reversal)
☐ Volume confirms the move
☐ Risk/Reward is at least 1.5:1 (preferably 2:1+)
☐ Stop-loss is placed based on technical structure
☐ Position size is calculated (1% rule)
☐ Profit target and exit plan are defined before entry
☐ No conflicting news or earnings imminent
```

### The Intraday Trader's Exit Checklist

```
☐ Hard stop-loss is in the system (not mental)
☐ If trade hits 1R profit, consider moving stop to breakeven
☐ If trade hits 2R+ profit, sell half, trail the rest
☐ Time-stop: close if no momentum in 20–25 minutes
☐ Exit on reversal candle at key level
☐ Exit if RSI hits extreme overbought/oversold
☐ Close ALL positions before market close
☐ After 3 losses, stop trading for the day
☐ After hitting daily loss limit (3%), shut down
```
